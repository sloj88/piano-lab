# Chapter 01 Music Specification — Meet the Piano

Status: v0.1 implementation-ready content proposal
Scope: Sessions 001–024
Companion documents: `chapter-01-content-spec.md` and `chapter-01-reinforcement-spec.md`

## Purpose and authority

This document is the single source of truth for Chapter 01 pitches, rhythms, fingerings, notation, and demonstration audio. It turns the approved curriculum outline into examples that can be typeset and independently checked. It does not change the approved sequence.

All examples are original for Piano Lab. Before publication, a qualified piano teacher should review the notated scores, fingerings, technique language, and recorded performances. The proposed content deliberately avoids pedal, simultaneous hands, accidentals, eighth notes, and stretches beyond a five-note position.

## Shared musical conventions

### Pitch names and keyboard positions

Scientific pitch notation is used in this specification so that implementers can identify an exact key. Learner-facing copy normally shows only the letter name once the position is established.

- **Middle C is C4.** It is the C nearest the center of an 88-key piano and the fourth C from the left. On a shorter keyboard it may not be physically centered; the two-black-key rule remains dependable.
- **Right-hand position:** C4–D4–E4–F4–G4 under fingers 1–2–3–4–5.
- **Left-hand position:** C3–D3–E3–F3–G3 under fingers 5–4–3–2–1.
- No exercise requires a key below C3 or above G4. A common 49-key portable keyboard can therefore support the chapter if it includes C3–G4.
- “Up” or “higher” means moving right on the keyboard; “down” or “lower” means moving left.

### Staff locations

| Pitch | Clef and written location | Hand / finger |
|---|---|---|
| C4 | Treble clef, one ledger line below the staff | RH 1 |
| D4 | Treble clef, space below the staff | RH 2 |
| E4 | Treble clef, bottom line | RH 3 |
| F4 | Treble clef, first space | RH 4 |
| G4 | Treble clef, second line | RH 5 |
| C3 | Bass clef, second space | LH 5 |
| D3 | Bass clef, middle line | LH 4 |
| E3 | Bass clef, third space | LH 3 |
| F3 | Bass clef, fourth line | LH 2 |
| G3 | Bass clef, fourth space | LH 1 |

The first staff-shape examples may suppress clefs and letter names so the learner can attend to repeat/up/down movement. Once a clef is introduced, every staff example must show it. Letter names must not appear above notes in reading challenges; accessible text alternatives may name the sequence after the learner chooses to reveal the answer.

From Session 015, a paired-staff layout shows the right-hand line above the left-hand line. Beat columns and barlines align vertically. Session 015 uses a hand-lane diagram first and presents paired staves only as a preview. Session 016 introduces a centered whole-measure rest as one practical sign meaning “this hand waits for all four beats”; it does not introduce or compare the whole-note value. Session 017 then teaches a quarter rest as exactly one silent beat. Session 016 labels every entrance `RH` or `LH` so the rest symbol is not the learner’s only hand cue.

### Rhythm and counting

- The continuing pulse is counted aloud as **“1, 2, 3, 4.”** A four-beat preparatory count is **“Ready: 1, 2, 3, 4.”**
- A quarter note lasts one beat. A half note lasts two beats and is counted across both numbers without replaying the key.
- A quarter rest is one silent beat; the count continues aloud or internally.
- From Session 005 onward, notated examples use **4/4**. Each measure contains four beats.
- A whole-measure rest appears from Session 016 only when one staff is inactive for all four beats. It is described as a full-measure waiting sign, not taught as a note value.
- Chapter 01 does not use whole notes, eighth notes, dotted values, ties, syncopation, pickup measures, changing meter, or pedal.
- The learner releases a half note at the end of its second beat unless the next note begins immediately; no note is held by a finger after its written duration.
- Finger numbers are shown only where they establish a position or solve a hand change. They are not substitutes for reading pitch.

### Tempo and performance guidance

Tempo markings are practice guidance, not pass/fail targets.

- Begin rhythm work around **52–60 beats per minute (bpm)**, or at a comfortable self-counted pulse.
- Most reading examples and pieces target **56–66 bpm**.
- A confident learner may try up to **72 bpm** only while the pulse, comfort, and intended rhythm remain clear.
- Audio files should include a spoken four-count but no continuous click. A separate optional metronome is not required for Chapter 01.
- “Approximately steady” means the learner can keep going without a conspicuous rush, long unplanned pause, or extra beat. Automated timing assessment is neither required nor permitted.

### Dynamics and physical approach

Session 019 introduces two relative dynamic levels: **soft (`p`, piano)** and **strong (`f`, forte)**. “Strong” never means hitting or forcing. The demonstration should show that a quicker, free key descent can make a fuller sound while shoulders, wrist, and jaw remain easy. The learner stops and resets if there is pain, numbness, or persistent tension.

Phrase shaping in Session 020 is taught through listening and plain language (“lead toward G, then settle back to C”). Crescendo and diminuendo symbols, slurs, articulation marks, and formal phrase-analysis terms are outside Chapter 01.

## Exercise and pattern library

Durations use `q` = quarter note, `h` = half note, and `qr` = quarter rest. A vertical bar separates measures. Pitches separated by spaces sound in sequence. Repeated pitches require a new key press.

### M001 — Published first melody

- **Sessions:** 001, 002, 019, 020, 023
- **Pitch/fingering:** RH C4(1) D4(2) E4(3) G4(5) E4(3) D4(2) C4(1)
- **Session 001 rhythm:** unmetered, one note per comfortable pulse, matching the published lesson.
- **From Session 020:** `4/4 | C4 q, D4 q, E4 h | G4 q, E4 q, D4 q, C4 q |`
- **Tempo:** 56–66 bpm after the learner can find the notes.
- **Notation:** Session 001 uses keyboard highlights and note/finger sequence. Session 020 adds treble staff notation and 4/4.
- **Audio:** optional reference in Session 020 only; not needed in Session 001.

### K001 — RH five-note walk

- **Sessions:** 001, 004, 023, 024
- **Pitch/fingering:** C4(1) D4(2) E4(3) F4(4) G4(5) F4(4) E4(3) D4(2) C4(1)
- **Rhythm:** unmetered in 001; one note per continuing beat in 004. Count cycles through 1–4 without stopping at G4.
- **Tempo:** 52–60 bpm.
- **Notation:** keyboard diagram first; no staff is required until the Chapter 01 challenge.

### K002 — LH five-note walk

- **Sessions:** 011, 012, 023, 024
- **Pitch/fingering:** C3(5) D3(4) E3(3) F3(2) G3(1) F3(2) E3(3) D3(4) C3(5)
- **Rhythm:** one note per continuing beat after a four-count.
- **Tempo:** 52–60 bpm.

### E001 — Same or different pitch pairs

- **Sessions:** 002, 024
- **Sound:** each note is a quarter-note-length tone at about 60 bpm; leave one beat between patterns.
- **Pair A (same):** C4 D4 E4 / C4 D4 E4.
- **Pair B (different):** C4 E4 D4 / C4 D4 E4.
- **Pair C (same):** G4 E4 C4 / G4 E4 C4.
- **Pair D (different, later review):** C3 D3 E3 / C3 E3 D3.
- **Audio:** required. Each pair needs Play again and Reveal controls. Do not show notation until the learner answers; the reveal should show the notes and direction.

### P001 — Sixteen steady beats

- **Session:** 003
- **Action:** 16 taps on leg, closed fallboard, or a safe non-key surface while counting four cycles of “1, 2, 3, 4.”
- **Tempo:** learner-chosen, approximately 52–72 bpm.
- **Visual:** 16 equally spaced pulse dots grouped in four sets of four. No staff notation.
- **Audio:** optional model count, not required.

### R001 — One- and two-beat durations

- **Sessions:** 005, 006, 018
- **On C4 first:** `| C4 q, C4 q, C4 h |`; `| C4 h, C4 q, C4 q |`; `| C4 q, C4 h, C4 q |`; `| C4 h, C4 h |`.
- **Finger:** RH 1. Later the patterns may alternate C4(1) and D4(2) without changing rhythm.
- **Tempo:** 52–60 bpm.
- **Audio:** spoken-count demonstration is useful in 005; rhythm echo audio is required in 006.

### R002 — Rhythm echo set

- **Session:** 006
- **Echo A:** `| C4 q, C4 q, C4 h |`.
- **Echo B:** `| C4 h, D4 q, C4 q |`.
- **Echo C:** `| C4 q, D4 h, C4 q |`.
- **Echo D (Keep Playing):** `| C4 q, D4 q, C4 q, D4 q | | C4 h, C4 h |`.
- **Method:** hear, tap, then play. The learner may replay twice before revealing count blocks.
- **Audio:** required, with a four-count before each pattern. Provide isolated pattern and pattern-plus-spoken-count versions.

### N001 — Staff contour cards

- **Session:** 007
- **Card A:** C4 C4 D4 E4 — repeat, repeat, up, up.
- **Card B:** E4 D4 D4 C4 — down, repeat, down.
- **Card C:** C4 D4 E4 D4 — up, up, down.
- **Rhythm:** four equal pulses; rhythm symbols may be quarter notes, but pitch direction is the task.
- **Notation:** neutral five-line staff without letter names; an optional reveal maps the notes to C4–E4.
- **Audio:** one optional contour example; visual and physical demonstration are sufficient.

### N002 — Treble landmarks

- **Sessions:** 008, 023, 024
- **Pattern A:** `| C4 q, G4 q, C4 h |`, RH 1–5–1.
- **Pattern B:** `| G4 q, C4 q, G4 h |`, RH 5–1–5.
- **Notation:** treble clef; C4 ledger line and G4 second line clearly enlarged. The leap is safe because the hand remains in C4–G4 position.
- **Tempo:** 52–60 bpm.

### N003 — Treble C–D–E reading cards

### E002 — Left-hand pitch echoes

- **Session:** 012
- **Echo A:** C3 D3 E3.
- **Echo B:** E3 D3 C3.
- **Echo C:** C3 C3 D3.
- **Rhythm:** three equal quarter-note-length tones after a count-in; no meter is assessed.
- **Audio:** required. Reveal shows arrow contour and then letter names.

### H001 — Hands-take-turns pattern

- **Session:** 015
- **Measure 1:** LH C3 q; RH C4 q; LH C3 q; RH C4 q.
- **Measure 2:** LH D3 q; RH D4 q; LH E3 q; RH E4 q.
- **Fingering:** LH C3(5), D3(4), E3(3); RH C4(1), D4(2), E4(3).
- **Notation:** begin with two labeled hand lanes and four beat columns. A paired-staff preview may follow; it must not be the sole explanation.
- **Tempo:** 48–56 bpm.
- **Audio:** optional; an animated beat pointer is sufficient.

### R003 — Quarter-rest patterns

- **Sessions:** 017, 018, 023
- **Pattern A:** `| C4 q, qr, D4 q, E4 q |`.
- **Pattern B:** `| C4 h, qr, C4 q |`.
- **Pattern C:** `| C3 q, D3 q, qr, E3 q |`.
- **Method:** speak “play, rest, play, play” while tapping, then count “1, 2, 3, 4,” then play.
- **Tempo:** 52–60 bpm.
- **Audio:** useful but not required if an animated count demonstrates the silent beat.

### R004 — Mixed-rhythm review cards

- **Session:** 018
- **Card A:** `| C4 q, C4 q, C4 h |`.
- **Card B:** `| C3 h, D3 q, E3 q |`.
- **Card C:** `| C4 q, qr, D4 q, E4 q |`.
- **Card D:** `| C3 q, D3 h, C3 q |`.
- **Card E (Keep Playing):** `| C4 q, D4 q, E4 q, D4 q | | C4 h, qr, C4 q |`.
- **Tempo:** 52–60 bpm.

### D001 — Soft and strong groups

- **Session:** 019
- **Pattern:** `| C4 q, C4 q, C4 q, C4 q |` at `p`, reset; repeat at `f`, never forced. Repeat on G3 with LH if comfortable.
- **Tempo:** 52–60 bpm.
- **Audio/video:** audio is required to establish relative contrast; a short close-up video or silent hand animation is strongly recommended to show an easy wrist and unforced motion. Text alone cannot model tone safely.

### C001 — Constrained musical answers

- **Session:** 020
- **Written question:** M001 in its two-measure rhythm.
- **Answer choices, RH only:**
  - A: `| E4 q, D4 q, C4 h |`
  - B: `| C4 q, D4 q, C4 h |`
  - C (learner-created): any three notes from C4–E4, with two quarter notes followed by a half note, ending on C4.
- **Audio:** required for one question-and-answer model. Choices should remain visible in notation so listening reinforces reading.

### Piece P03 — “Across the Keys” (Chapter 01 final piece)

- **Sessions:** 021–024

- **Range:** C3–G3 and C4–G4
- **Coordination:** one hand at a time, except that Measure 7 changes hand between beats; no notes sound simultaneously
- **Dynamics:** `p` for Measures 1–4; `f` for Measures 5–8, interpreted as warm/strong rather than forced

| Measure | Active hand(s) | Notes and durations | Fingering |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | RH | C4 q, D4 q, E4 h | 1, 2, 3 |
| 2 | RH | E4 q, D4 q, C4 h | 3, 2, 1 |
| 3 | LH | C3 q, D3 q, E3 h | 5, 4, 3 |
| 4 | LH | E3 q, D3 q, C3 h | 3, 4, 5 |
| 5 | RH | C4 q, E4 q, G4 h | 1, 3, 5 |
| 6 | LH | C3 q, E3 q, G3 h | 5, 3, 1 |
| 7 | LH then RH | C3 q, qr, C4 q, D4 q | LH 5; RH 1, 2 |
| 8 | RH | E4 q, D4 q, C4 h | 3, 2, 1 |

#### Typesetting requirements

- Use paired treble and bass staves throughout, with aligned barlines and generous spacing.
- Put one centered whole-measure rest in an inactive hand’s full measure. In Measure 7, show LH C3 on beat 1 followed by three quarter rests in bass; show two quarter rests in treble followed by RH C4 and D4 on beats 3 and 4.
- Add `RH` at Measure 1, `LH` at Measure 3, `RH` at Measure 5, `LH` at Measure 6, and both entrance labels in Measure 7. Do not rely on stem direction or color to identify hands.
- Place `p` at Measure 1 and `f` at Measure 5. Include the plain-language key “p = soft; f = strong, never forced.”
- Show finger numbers at Measures 1, 3, 5, 6, and both Measure 7 entrances; omit redundant numbers elsewhere.
- Phrase layout should visually group Measures 1–4 and 5–8, preferably four measures per system on desktop and two per system on narrow screens. Never shrink staff notation below a comfortably readable size; horizontal scrolling within the score is preferable to illegible scaling.

#### Preparation plan

1. Tap the rhythm of Measures 1–4, then 5–8, counting every beat and rest.
2. Play RH Measures 1–2 and 5, then RH beats 3–4 of Measure 7 through Measure 8.
3. Play LH Measures 3–4 and 6, then beat 1 of Measure 7.
4. Loop Measures 6–7 at 48–52 bpm using “LH, hold; LH, rest; RH, RH” as a temporary cue.
5. Connect Measures 1–4, then Measures 5–8. Finally play all eight after a four-count.
6. Add soft/strong contrast only after the hand changes and durations are dependable.

The final piece uses only skills introduced by Session 020. Measure 7 is the single highest-coordination moment and receives isolated practice in Session 021. It contains a hand change but not simultaneous hands.

Reference audio is required for the polished target, but it should be offered after score preview. Provide: (1) rhythm plus count at 52 bpm, (2) hands/phrases separately at 52 bpm, and (3) a complete musical performance at 60–64 bpm. A moving beat cursor is useful but optional.

## Demonstration and audio requirements

All audio is locally bundled, short, and usable offline after the page loads. It must not contain copyrighted repertoire. Controls need descriptive labels, replay support, visible duration/state, and no autoplay. Spoken count-ins and instruction should have transcripts; musical examples should have text alternatives listing their notes and rhythm after the task is complete. Volume must never jump unexpectedly between files.

| Learning need | Examples | Audio status | Rationale / presentation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Same/different pitch | E001 | **Required** | The target skill is auditory comparison. Hide the answer until response; allow replay. |
| Rhythm echoes | R002 | **Required** | An echo must be heard before it is reproduced. Offer pattern-only and counted versions. |
| Left-hand pitch echoes | E002 | **Required** | The learner copies pitch direction by ear, then checks it against contour and names. |
| Short melody and phrase shape | M001, C001 | **Required in 020; optional earlier** | Audio models direction and musical destination while notation stays visible. |
| Dynamic contrast | D001 | **Required** | “Soft” and “strong” are sound concepts; audio plus motion modeling supports safe technique. |
| Complete pieces | P01, P02, P03 | **Useful for P01/P02; required for P03** | Let the learner preview the score first. Audio is a reference, not a play-along dependency. |
| Pulse and note durations | P001, R001, R003 | **Useful, not required** | Count blocks, animation, and physical tapping can teach these; audio improves clarity but is not essential. |
| Keyboard geography, finger numbers, staff landmarks | K001/K002, N002/N004 | **Not required** | Accurate diagrams, close-up motion, and text provide the needed information. |

No example listens to the learner. There is no microphone permission, MIDI connection, virtual playable keyboard, automated score, or cloud request. The browser demonstrates; the learner plays the physical instrument and evaluates the result.

## Accessibility and device requirements

- Scores need scalable vector or similarly crisp notation, text alternatives, and a zoom or enlarged view. Pitch, hand, rhythm, and status cannot be conveyed by color alone.
- Keyboard diagrams must preserve two- and three-black-key geometry and label the visible register. On narrow phones, allow horizontal score/keyboard scrolling rather than distorting key widths or compressing notation.
- Every audio control must be keyboard operable and at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels. Replay must not erase a learner’s response.
- Audio is supportive. Every non-auditory concept remains teachable with notation, diagrams, counting, and physical action; genuinely auditory tasks have a clear “audio unavailable” explanation and may be revisited without losing progress.
- Acoustic pianos and portable keyboards may differ in touch, volume control, and key count. Dynamic guidance should tell portable-keyboard users to choose a moderate device volume and still vary touch only if the instrument is touch-sensitive. If it is not touch-sensitive, they can compare relaxed key movement and use the volume exercise as listening-only.

## Internal-consistency and educator-review checklist

- Every pitch is within C3–G4 and every fingering fits one fixed five-note hand position.
- Played rhythms require only quarter notes, half notes, and quarter rests; a whole-measure rest marks a hand that waits for an entire measure from Session 016 onward.
- Meter is 4/4 whenever barlines are used, and every listed measure totals four beats.
- No task before Session 015 changes hands inside a pulse; no task in the chapter requires simultaneous notes.
- Ear work always reveals or reconnects to notation/contour after listening.
- All pieces have a clear ending and can be played without pedal.
- The 8-measure final piece uses only skills introduced by Session 020.
- A qualified teacher must review the C4/C3 five-finger positions for varied adult hand sizes; the “level wrist” shorthand in published Session 001; dynamic-production wording; the Measure 6–7 change in P03; all notation engraving; and all demonstration performances before implementation is accepted.

## Proposed clarification, not a baseline change

The approved outline describes the Session 006 echoes as “two-measure echo patterns,” while its core objective can be met more safely with the one-measure R002 patterns before the optional two-measure Echo D. This specification treats the phrase as a maximum example length, not a requirement that every echo span two measures. No approved curriculum document has been edited; reviewers should confirm this interpretation.
