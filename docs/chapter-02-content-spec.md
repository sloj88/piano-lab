# Chapter 02 Content Specification — Read and Feel the Beat

Status: draft for teacher review and adult-beginner trials

## Shared lesson contract

Every row is one session. The first column gives core minutes as **preview or retrieval / explain or model / physical practice / reflection**. During an independent attempt, use the model minutes for silent planning and offer the model afterward where specified. Allow repeating a step or moving on with a specific revisit note. Each lesson ends with independent understanding and playing self-checks and Ready, Practicing or Stuck. The support codes link to the [reinforcement specification](chapter-02-reinforcement-spec.md). Optional Keep Playing happens after the core. Exact music is in the [music specification](chapter-02-music-spec.md).

**Preview routine.** Point to clef and meter, locate the first note by landmark and direction, scan for one change in rhythm or contour, then briefly look down to place the starting finger and find a comfortable position. Touch silently without pressing. Return eyes to the first bar, count one preparatory bar and start. During playing, use brief glances where useful. After a lost place, release and restart at a barline after a fresh count. Fingering help is specific to the first key or difficult location. No fixed gaze, unsupported stretching, or forced sound. Pause on discomfort and stop on pain, numbness or persistent tension.

**Rhythm practice.** Speak the numbers first, tap attacks against a steady count, and play on the physical instrument. Count through holds and rests. Use a small hand hover or visual cell for sustain during tapping since a tap itself cannot sustain. For eighth pairs, split one beat into two equal parts before adding pitch. In early examples use a spoken model with no continuous click. Use a short optional repeat and keep help closed for the first attempt in review sessions.

**Prerequisite map for implementation.** Sequence all sessions in outline order. Record these instructional dependencies where the preceding session alone would miss the source skill: 025 uses 008, 013, 016 and 024; 026 builds on 025; 027–028 use 026 and the respective landmark sessions 008 and 013; 029 retrieves both 027 and 028. Sessions 030–032 use Chapter 01 duration work (005), with 031 also using 017 and 030. Session 033 uses the 4/4 preparation of 026 and the waiting sign from 016. Session 034 combines 030, 032 and 033. Session 035 uses 028 and 032. Sessions 036–037 use the counted silence of 017, with 037 also using 036. Sessions 038–041 use 030–031, and each eighth-pair transfer follows 038. Session 042 uses 030, 033 and 039’s counted barline; 043 uses 035 and 042. Session 044 draws on 036, 039–040 and 043. Window Lights uses 027, 032 and 019; Footpath uses 028, 031, 039–040 and 019. Performances 046 and 048 follow their own preparation sessions. Session 049 uses 029, 035 and 044. Session 050 follows both performances. Session 051 follows 049 and retrieves the repair routine from 046/048. Session 052 follows the reading rehearsal and both performances. These dependencies guide lesson design and support links.

### Critical review and trial notes

- **Pacing.** New meters, a whole note, and multi-clef retrieval occur within 030–035. Test whether readers are counting from the signature or copying the model. If that cluster feels crowded, use one-bar retries and revisit earlier meter cards before adding length. Keep the final core near 14 minutes by limiting initial attempts to one per task and offering extended repairs separately.
- **Reading fluency.** The reader performs newly encountered lines in 027–029 and again in 049, 051, and 052. In trials, observe whether they derive the starting pitch from the staff, follow contour, and recover a place after a glance. Repeated success with memorized pieces is separate evidence.
- **Hands and repertoire.** Both hands read stepwise cards, attempt mixed retrieval, and receive one eight-bar piece. RH introduces subdivision and tying first, then LH explicitly transfers each. The LH piece is the more rhythmically dense piece, and the preparation tempo is lower. Trial the bass transitions in 028, 039→040, and Footpath bars 1 and 5 for strain or overload.
- **Independent success.** Use a single helpful hint, a matched example and a short retry. Avoid adding mandatory repetitions. An honest milestone attempt and specific next practice target count as core engagement. No self-rating gates progress.
- **Milestone validity.** The unseen clef choice gives equally long, stepwise 3/4 lines with the same pitch inventory and familiar note values. The RH card adds a familiar quarter rest, so verify the choices feel comparable in trials. The rhythm card independently probes silent pulse and subdivision. Previously prepared pieces check continuity and character. Ties are checked separately in 042–044, outside the milestone’s sight-playing task.
- **Educator review.** Ask a qualified piano teacher to review range, fingerings, position and repeated-note comfort for varied adult hands, tie and rest examples, first-read equivalence, manageable tempi, shaping and dynamics, and the two complete scores and recordings. Observe adults learning independently and ask an accessibility reviewer to check enlargement, keyboard focus and timing descriptions. These drafts are proposals for those reviews.
