# Chapter 01 Content Specification — Meet the Piano

Status: v0.1 implementation-ready content proposal
Scope: Sessions 001–024
Baseline: approved curriculum architecture v0.2

## How to use this specification

This document supplies the learner-facing teaching sequence for every Chapter 01 session. Exact pitches, rhythms, scores, and audio are defined by ID in `chapter-01-music-spec.md`; short supports are defined in `chapter-01-reinforcement-spec.md`. Together, the three documents are intended to prevent musical content from being invented during implementation.

The learner uses a physical piano or keyboard. On-screen keys are diagrams, never playable substitutes. Each core is designed for 10–15 minutes; **Keep Playing** is optional and does not affect completion. A learner may repeat, remain, revisit, or continue. Completing a session, understanding it, playing it, and feeling ready are separate facts.

### Reusable session ending

Unless a session specifies a more focused prompt, finish with these controls:

- **Understanding:** “I understand the idea” / “I want the explanation again.”
- **Playing:** “I can do the playing task” / “I’m still working on it.”
- **Readiness:** **Ready**, **Practicing**, or **Stuck**.

**Ready** offers Continue and Keep Playing. **Practicing** keeps the learner at the session and offers its named repeat. **Stuck** reveals a simpler prerequisite and a link to the named earlier session. None of these choices erases completion or later history.

### Technique language used throughout

The learner should sit at a height and distance that allow the forearms to feel roughly level with the keys, shoulders to rest, wrists to move freely, and fingers to curve naturally rather than collapse or stretch. A portable keyboard needs a stable surface. The learner should pause, release the keys, and gently reset whenever the hand, wrist, shoulder, neck, or jaw becomes tight. Pain, tingling, or numbness means stop; this course is not medical advice.

## Session specifications

### 001 — Your First Five Notes · Learn

- **Primary objective:** Use the black-key pattern to find middle C, identify C–G, place RH fingers 1–5, and attempt the first melody.
- **Prerequisites:** A physical keyboard and no musical knowledge.
- **Music references:** K001 and M001.

**Instructional explanation**

> The long keyboard is made from one small pattern repeated many times. The black keys come in groups of two and three. These groups are landmarks. Find two black keys: the white key immediately to their left is C. Middle C is the C nearest the middle of a standard piano; on a smaller keyboard it may sit a little away from the physical center.
>
> Starting on middle C, the next white keys to the right are D, E, F, and G. Right-hand fingers are numbered from thumb to little finger: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. Rest those fingers on C, D, E, F, G. Your first melody is C–D–E–G–E–D–C, using 1–2–3–5–3–2–1.

**Demonstration:** Preserve the published five-step diagrams and highlighted C4–G4 keys. Show two/three black-key groups, C immediately left of a two-key group, RH numbering, K001, and M001. The diagram must state that it is a reference for the physical instrument.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Point to two groups of two black keys and two groups of three on the instrument.
2. Find C beside two different two-black-key groups; then find C4.
3. Play C4–G4 one key at a time while saying the names.
4. Place RH 1–5 on C4–G4. Release, shake out gently, and reset if the wrist or shoulder tightens.
5. Play one K001 ascent and descent at any calm speed.
6. Say M001, tap its finger numbers on the leg, then make one complete playing attempt.

- **Visual/notation:** Existing accurate keyboard diagrams, RH finger diagram, and note/finger sequence. Standard staff notation is not required yet.
- **Time budget:** landmarks 2 min; C and C4 2 min; notes 2 min; hand setup/K001 3 min; M001 3 min; reflection 1 min = **13 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Finds C4 using the two-black-key landmark and completes a comfortable attempt at M001.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I explain how a two-black-key group helps me find C?” Playing—“Can I place RH 1–5 and attempt the whole melody without pain?”
- **Practicing:** Repeat only “say, tap fingers, play” for M001 twice.
- **Stuck:** Cover part of the keyboard, find one two-black-key group, and locate only C; use reinforcement **RF-KG-1**.
- **Keep Playing:** Find more Cs, repeat K001, try M001 from memory, or play it once softly and once at a comfortable medium sound.

### 002 — Find and Hear It Again · Review

- **Primary objective:** Retrieve the Session 001 landmarks and distinguish tiny pitch patterns that are the same or different.
- **Prerequisites:** Session 001 attempt; perfection is not required.
- **Music references:** E001 pairs A–C and M001.

**Instructional explanation**

> Remembering grows when you try before reopening the answer. First use the keyboard’s shape: two black keys lead you to C. Then listen to two short groups of notes. “Same” means every sound arrives in the same order. “Different” means at least one sound changes. You do not need to name the notes to hear the difference.

**Demonstration:** Give one non-scored same example and one different example, then reveal the directions and note names. Explain Play again and Reveal; do not imply that fewer replays are better.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Without the diagram, point to two two-black-key groups, find two Cs, and choose C4.
2. Place RH on C4–G4 and play M001 from memory once; reveal the sequence afterward if needed.
3. Hear E001 A–C. For each pair, choose Same or Different, then reveal and play both patterns on the physical keys.
4. Name whether the main difficulty was remembering the rule, hearing the change, or moving the fingers.

- **Visual/notation:** Collapsed keyboard hint; audio controls; reveal cards with direction arrows and C4–G4 highlights.
- **Time budget:** retrieval 3 min; melody 2 min; three listening pairs 5 min; play/check 2 min; reflection 1 min = **13 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Restarts M001 independently and correctly distinguishes at least two of three pairs after optional replay.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Do I know what same and different mean for a note pattern?” Playing—“Can I find the notes and check a heard pattern on my piano?”
- **Practicing:** Replay one missed pair, sing or hum its direction if comfortable, and play each version slowly.
- **Stuck:** Compare only two single notes, C4/C4 and C4/D4; use **RF-EAR-1** and revisit Session 001’s Find C step if needed.
- **Keep Playing:** Start M001 on another C with the same white-key shape and describe whether it sounds higher or lower.

### 003 — Feel a Steady Pulse · Learn

- **Primary objective:** Experience pulse as evenly spaced musical time.
- **Prerequisites:** None beyond safe seating.
- **Music reference:** P001.

**Instructional explanation**

> A pulse is music’s steady background beat. It keeps going even when the notes change or stop. Count “1, 2, 3, 4” at an easy walking speed. Each number should receive the same amount of time. The numbers organize the pulse; they are not finger numbers here.

**Demonstration:** Animate 16 evenly spaced dots while a calm voice counts four groups of four. Contrast it once with an intentionally rushed final group, clearly labeled “not steady.”

**Core physical exercise**

1. Keep hands away from the keys. Tap four slow, even beats on a leg or closed fallboard.
2. Repeat four times without stopping: 16 taps total, counting 1–4 each time.
3. Rest for four silent counts, then restart at approximately the same speed.
4. Play C4 once on each of eight new beats with RH 1; listen without trying to be fast.

- **Visual/notation:** P001 pulse dots; no staff. Include a warning not to tap on a fallboard that could close or move.
- **Time budget:** explanation/model 2 min; tapping 3 min; silent/restart task 2 min; key task 3 min; repeat 2 min; reflection 1 min = **13 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Produces 16 approximately even taps while counting and restarts without a conspicuous speed change.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I describe pulse as steady time, not the notes themselves?” Playing—“Can I match one key press to each beat?”
- **Practicing:** Use eight beats instead of 16, rest, and repeat three times.
- **Stuck:** Let the optional model count guide four taps, then mute it and copy four; use **RF-PULSE-1**.
- **Keep Playing:** Walk or sway safely with a favorite recording and notice its continuing pulse; no beat identification score is required.

### 004 — Five Notes, One Beat Each · Practice

- **Primary objective:** Coordinate one RH note with each continuing beat through a direction change.
- **Prerequisites:** Find C4, place RH 1–5, and tap four even beats.
- **Music reference:** K001.

**Instructional explanation**

> Today the pulse keeps moving while the notes go up and down. Count 1–4 again and again. G may arrive on a different count each time; that is fine. Do not add a pause at G. A slower, even pattern teaches more than a fast, uneven one.

**Demonstration:** Show a beat cursor advancing evenly through K001, continuing through G4. Model a relaxed reset before the count-in.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Tap eight beats and say the repeating count.
2. Place RH 1–5 on C4–G4 and make a silent comfort check.
3. Count four preparation beats; play K001 one note per beat without stopping at G4.
4. Pause and name one observation: pulse, correct key, or comfort.
5. Repeat twice, changing only that one item.

- **Visual/notation:** Keyboard/finger map plus nine equal beat cells; no staff reading demand.
- **Time budget:** pulse reset 2 min; setup 2 min; three attempts 6 min; targeted repair 2 min; reflection 1 min = **13 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Plays K001 twice without an extra pause at G4 or a marked rush downward.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Do I know why the count continues through the turn?” Playing—“Can my fingers follow that pulse with an easy hand?”
- **Practicing:** Repeat only E4–F4–G4–F4–E4 on five even beats, then restore K001.
- **Stuck:** Return to Session 003’s eight C4 presses; then play C4–D4 only. Use **RF-RH-1**.
- **Keep Playing:** Try K001 at a deliberately slower pulse and compare control, not speed.

### 005 — One Beat or Two · Learn

- **Primary objective:** Perform and read quarter notes and half notes as one- and two-beat sounds.
- **Prerequisites:** Count a steady four-beat pulse.
- **Music reference:** R001.

**Instructional explanation**

> A quarter note lasts for one beat. A half note lasts for two beats. During a half note, press once and let the sound continue while you count the second number. Do not press the key again. The pulse never waits for the note.

**Demonstration:** On C4, show and play R001’s first and fourth patterns with animated count blocks. Make the release at the end of the duration visible.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Count and tap four quarter-note beats.
2. Press C4 once and hold through counts 1–2; release before count 3. Repeat on 3–4.
3. Speak, tap, then play each R001 pattern on C4.
4. Play the first pattern once more using C4, D4, E4 for its three sounds while preserving the durations.

- **Visual/notation:** Large quarter and half note with one/two beat blocks; 4/4 measures; C4 keyboard highlight.
- **Time budget:** explanation/model 3 min; isolated duration 2 min; four patterns 5 min; pitch transfer 2 min; reflection 1 min = **13 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Presses once for a half note and releases after two counted beats in at least two patterns.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I explain the difference between one beat and two beats?” Playing—“Can I keep counting while a sound continues?”
- **Practicing:** Alternate four C4 quarter notes with two C4 half notes.
- **Stuck:** Tap the durations with no piano and shade one box for each beat; use **RF-RHY-1**.
- **Keep Playing:** Create one four-beat measure from quarter and half notes, then repeat it exactly.

### 006 — Rhythm Echoes · Practice

- **Primary objective:** Hear, tap, and play short one- and two-beat rhythm patterns.
- **Prerequisites:** Quarter/half durations and steady count.
- **Music reference:** R002.

**Instructional explanation**

> An echo repeats what it hears. Listen to the whole rhythm before moving. Then tap it, count it, and finally play it. The note may change, but the timing is the part you are copying. Replay is a practice tool, not a penalty.

**Demonstration:** Model Hear → Tap → Play for R002 A. Reveal the count blocks only after the first attempt.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Hear R002 A; tap and play it on C4.
2. Repeat B and C, using C4/D4 as written.
3. Choose the least secure pattern, reveal its count, and play it twice the same way.
4. Finish by explaining where the two-beat note occurred.

- **Visual/notation:** Audio/replay controls, hidden-then-revealed score/count blocks, C4/D4 key diagram.
- **Time budget:** model 2 min; three echoes 6 min; repair 3 min; reflection 1 min = **12 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Reproduces at least three patterns after no more than two listens each and can point to a half note.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I hear which sound lasts two beats?” Playing—“Can I copy the rhythm without adding an extra press?”
- **Practicing:** Loop one pattern as Listen → Tap → Play three times.
- **Stuck:** Use R001’s all-C4 pattern with visible counts; use **RF-RHY-2** or revisit Session 005.
- **Keep Playing:** Try R002 D, then invent a one-measure echo with C4/D4.

### 007 — The Staff Shows Shape · Learn

- **Primary objective:** Connect written repeat/up/down shape to same/right/left key movement.
- **Prerequisites:** Recognize higher/right and lower/left on C4–E4.
- **Music reference:** N001.

**Instructional explanation**

> Music is read from left to right. The five horizontal lines are called a staff. A note placed higher on the staff sounds higher and moves to the right on this part of the keyboard. A note placed lower sounds lower and moves left. Notes at the same height repeat the same key. Today, follow shape; note names come next.

**Demonstration:** Trace N001 A from left to right, saying “repeat, up, up,” then play C4 C4 D4 E4. Show one note moving from a line to the neighboring space.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Point left-to-right across each N001 card.
2. Say each movement: repeat, up, or down.
3. Place RH over C4–E4 and play the card’s shape using adjacent keys.
4. Hear one card and choose its matching written shape; reveal and replay it.

- **Visual/notation:** Enlarged five-line staff, direction arrows on demand, N001 A–C. Do not color-code the only answer cue.
- **Time budget:** explanation/model 3 min; three trace/play cards 6 min; sound-to-shape match 2 min; reflection 1 min = **12 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Correctly maps repeat/up/down notation to same/right/left motion on at least two unfamiliar cards.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I explain what higher and lower placement means?” Playing—“Can I follow a four-note shape on neighboring keys?”
- **Practicing:** Use only two-note cards: same, one step up, one step down.
- **Stuck:** Move a marker vertically between two large staff positions, then mirror it on C4/D4; use **RF-READ-1**.
- **Keep Playing:** Draw or arrange a four-note contour, play it, then change one note and compare.

### 008 — Two Treble Landmarks · Learn

- **Primary objective:** Recognize written C4 and G4 and connect them to RH keys.
- **Prerequisites:** Staff shape and C4/G4 keyboard location.
- **Music reference:** N002.

**Instructional explanation**

> The treble clef marks the staff used for these right-hand notes. Middle C sits on one short line below the staff. That short line belongs only to the note and is called a ledger line. G sits on the second line up from the bottom. These two landmarks let you find nearby notes without memorizing a sentence.

**Demonstration:** Show C4 and G4 appearing on the treble staff and then highlight the physical keys. Count staff lines from the bottom for G4 once; thereafter emphasize visual location.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Find and play C4 with RH 1 and G4 with RH 5.
2. Point to written C4/G4 in a mixed set, then play each answer.
3. Preview and play N002 A and B after a four-count.
4. Hide labels and repeat one pattern.

- **Visual/notation:** Treble clef, enlarged ledger line, N002, matching keyboard diagram; text labels available as a reveal.
- **Time budget:** explanation/model 3 min; matching 3 min; patterns 4 min; no-label check 2 min; reflection 1 min = **13 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Identifies written C4 and G4 without a mnemonic and plays the corresponding keys.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I describe where both landmarks sit?” Playing—“Can I move between them without shifting the whole hand?”
- **Practicing:** Shuffle four C4/G4 cards and answer slowly.
- **Stuck:** Match only written C4 to its key, then add G4; use **RF-READ-2**.
- **Keep Playing:** Find another C and G on the keyboard and compare their higher/lower register; staff reading stays at C4/G4.

### 009 — Read C, D, E · Practice

- **Primary objective:** Read stepwise C4–D4–E4 patterns in treble clef.
- **Prerequisites:** C4 landmark, staff direction, quarter notes.
- **Music reference:** N003 A–C.

**Instructional explanation**

> Start from the middle-C landmark. The next note up is D, in the space below the staff. The next step is E, on the bottom line. Before playing, find the first note and trace whether the pattern repeats, steps up, or steps down. Reading the shape is more useful than writing every letter above it.

**Demonstration:** Preview N003 A: landmark, direction, rhythm, hand position, count-in. Then play without cursor-following assistance.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Locate C4, D4, and E4 on staff and keyboard.
2. For N003 A, speak directions, count four, and play.
3. Repeat with B and C; show names only after each attempt.
4. Revisit one card and play it without note labels or moving cursor.

- **Visual/notation:** Treble cards, optional direction overlay, score reveal, RH starting finger only.
- **Time budget:** mapping 2 min; preview method 2 min; three cards 6 min; independent repeat 2 min; reflection 1 min = **13 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Reads a new four-note C4–E4 pattern without letter names above notes.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I use C4 plus step direction to find D4 and E4?” Playing—“Can I keep the RH position while reading?”
- **Practicing:** Alternate cards A and B, pausing to preview each.
- **Stuck:** Use a two-note C4/D4 card and **RF-TREBLE-1**; revisit Session 007 if direction is unclear.
- **Keep Playing:** Move the same physical key shape to D4–E4–F4 by ear/shape only; this is not a notation-reading test.

### 010 — A Phrase from the Page · Play

- **Primary objective:** Prepare and play the complete four-measure RH piece P01 from notation.
- **Prerequisites:** Treble C4–E4; quarter and half notes; count-in.
- **Music reference:** P01 “Three-Step Walk.”

**Instructional explanation**

> A piece has a beginning, a journey, and an ending. Preview before you play: find the first note, trace the note shape, notice one- and two-beat notes, and prepare your hand. If a note is missed, keep the pulse moving when you can. Then repair that place after the complete attempt.

**Demonstration:** Visually model the four-step preview and perform Measures 1–2 only. Keep full reference audio behind “Hear after my try.”

**Core physical exercise**

1. Preview P01 without playing.
2. Tap/count Measures 1–2, then play them; repeat with Measures 3–4.
3. Count in and play all four measures.
4. Identify one useful repair, practice that measure once, and replay the piece.

- **Visual/notation:** Full P01 treble score, measure numbers, enlarged mode, optional beat blocks, audio after first attempt.
- **Time budget:** preview 2 min; sections 4 min; full attempt 2 min; repair/replay 3 min; reflection 1 min = **12 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Completes P01 from notation with recognizable rhythm and ending.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I show how I preview a piece?” Playing—“Can I reach the ending without restarting for every small error?”
- **Practicing:** Loop only the least secure measure three times, then connect it to the next measure.
- **Stuck:** Play only Measures 1–2 using **RF-PIECE-1**, or revisit N003 A.
- **Keep Playing:** Replay with a chosen title/character, then hear the reference and compare one musical detail rather than correctness alone.

### 011 — Meet the Left Hand · Learn

- **Primary objective:** Establish a comfortable LH C3–G3 five-note position and finger numbering.
- **Prerequisites:** Keyboard C rule and general comfort setup.
- **Music reference:** K002.

**Instructional explanation**

> Left-hand numbers also begin at the thumb: thumb 1, then 2, 3, 4, and little finger 5. Find the C one octave below middle C: it is the next C to the left. Place LH 5 on C3 and 4–3–2–1 on D–E–F–G. The fingers may rest near the keys; do not stretch them into a rigid row.

**Demonstration:** Show C4, travel to the next C left (C3), and place LH 5–1. Model release–move–prepare–play and a neutral, mobile wrist.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Find C4, then the next C to the left, C3.
2. Name and gently move LH fingers 1–5 away from the keyboard.
3. Place LH 5–1 on C3–G3; release and reset once.
4. Play K002 slowly, pausing after the ascent if needed.
5. Repeat only while shoulder, wrist, and fingertips remain comfortable.

- **Visual/notation:** Keyboard octave map, LH finger diagram viewed from learner position, K002 key highlights; no bass staff yet.
- **Time budget:** C3 landmark 2 min; numbering/setup 4 min; K002 attempts 4 min; comfort check 2 min; reflection 1 min = **13 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Finds C3, names LH fingers, and plays K002 without forced stretching.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Do I know why LH little finger is 5?” Playing—“Can I place and move the hand without pain or held tension?”
- **Practicing:** Play only C3–D3–E3 and back with LH 5–4–3.
- **Stuck:** Locate C3 and play it four relaxed times with LH 5; use **RF-LH-1**.
- **Keep Playing:** Play K002 softly and listen for an even, unforced tone.

### 012 — Left-Hand Steps and Echoes · Practice

- **Primary objective:** Keep a pulse with LH and reproduce a three-note heard contour.
- **Prerequisites:** Comfortable LH C3–G3 position and steady pulse.
- **Music references:** K002 and E002.

**Instructional explanation**

> The left hand learns the same way as the right: prepare the position, use a slow pulse, and listen. In the echo, first decide whether the sound repeats, moves right/up, or moves left/down. Then check that shape with C, D, and E.

**Demonstration:** Play K002 with a count and model E002 A as Hear → trace direction → play.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Count in and play K002 one note per beat twice.
2. Play two visual C3–E3 step patterns with LH.
3. Hear and reproduce E002 A–C, revealing contour after each attempt.
4. Repeat the least secure item once after a full hand release.

- **Visual/notation:** LH key/finger map, simple direction arrows, E002 audio/reveal; bass notation is not required until Session 013.
- **Time budget:** K002 3 min; visual patterns 3 min; echoes 4 min; targeted repeat 2 min; reflection 1 min = **13 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Plays two LH patterns evenly and reproduces at least one three-note echo.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I connect heard up/down movement to the keyboard?” Playing—“Can my LH keep an easy shape and clear releases?”
- **Practicing:** Repeat E002 A with visible arrows, then hide them.
- **Stuck:** Use C3/D3 only and **RF-LH-2**; return to Session 011 if placement is uncertain.
- **Keep Playing:** Hear a C4–E4 RH pattern and copy its shape one octave lower with LH.

### 013 — Bass C and Bass F · Learn

- **Primary objective:** Recognize C3 and F3 as bass-clef landmarks.
- **Prerequisites:** Find C3/F3 in LH position and understand staff line/space placement.
- **Music reference:** N004.

**Instructional explanation**

> The bass clef marks the staff used for these lower left-hand notes. C3 sits in the second space from the bottom. F3 sits on the fourth line from the bottom. They are already under LH fingers 5 and 2. These two landmarks help you read nearby notes without decoding every line and space from scratch.

**Demonstration:** Animate written C3/F3 to their keys and fingers, then preview N004 A.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Find C3 with LH 5 and F3 with LH 2.
2. Match six mixed C3/F3 notation cards to the key, playing each answer.
3. Count in and play N004 A and B.
4. Hide names and identify one final card.

- **Visual/notation:** Bass clef, enlarged second space/fourth line, keyboard link, N004.
- **Time budget:** explanation/model 3 min; card matching 4 min; patterns 3 min; no-label check 1 min; reflection 1 min = **12 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Matches written C3 and F3 to the correct key/finger without a letter mnemonic.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I describe both bass landmarks?” Playing—“Can I play them without moving out of the five-note position?”
- **Practicing:** Shuffle four cards and answer with no time limit.
- **Stuck:** Learn written C3 alone, then add F3; use **RF-BASS-1**.
- **Keep Playing:** Find the C3/F3 landmarks in two new short repeat patterns.

### 014 — Read C, D, E with the Left Hand · Practice

- **Primary objective:** Read stepwise C3–D3–E3 patterns in bass clef.
- **Prerequisites:** Bass C3 landmark, staff direction, quarter notes.
- **Music reference:** N005 A–C.

**Instructional explanation**

> Begin from bass C. The next step up is D on the middle line; the next is E in the space above it. On this keyboard area, a written step up still moves one white key to the right. Preview the landmark and shape before the fingers move.

**Demonstration:** Use N005 A to model landmark → direction → rhythm → count-in, with only the starting finger displayed.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Match written C3/D3/E3 to LH 5/4/3.
2. Preview, count, and play N005 A.
3. Repeat with B and C; reveal names only after each attempt.
4. Replay one card without note labels.

- **Visual/notation:** Bass cards, optional direction overlay, keyboard/finger reveal.
- **Time budget:** mapping 2 min; modeled preview 2 min; three cards 6 min; repeat/reflection 2 min = **12 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Reads a new four-note C3–E3 pattern while keeping a comfortable LH position.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I use bass C plus steps to find D and E?” Playing—“Can I read without tightening or looking back and forth for every note?”
- **Practicing:** Alternate cards A/B with a silent preview before each.
- **Stuck:** Use C3/D3 only with **RF-BASS-2**; revisit Session 013 if the landmark is uncertain.
- **Keep Playing:** Read a short F3-starting step pattern using contour only; reveal its letters afterward.

### 015 — Hands Take Turns · Learn

- **Primary objective:** Change active hands on a continuing pulse without simultaneous playing.
- **Prerequisites:** RH and LH C–E positions and four-beat pulse.
- **Music reference:** H001.

**Instructional explanation**

> Both hands will share one pulse, but they do not play together. Prepare LH C below middle C and RH middle C before starting. One hand plays; the other waits near its keys. The waiting hand stays easy rather than lifting high. Keep counting through every change.

**Demonstration:** Use two labeled hand lanes and four beat columns to show LH–RH–LH–RH. Demonstrate the switch slowly. Then preview how the same timing is aligned on two staves, without testing that notation yet.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Prepare LH C3 and RH C4; silently touch each finger to confirm location.
2. Tap LH–RH–LH–RH on the legs for four beats.
3. Play H001 Measure 1 at 48–56 bpm.
4. Add Measure 2 separately, then connect both after a count-in.
5. Release both hands and check shoulders/wrists.

- **Visual/notation:** Labeled hand-lane diagram first; paired-staff preview second; beat cursor and explicit hand text, never color alone.
- **Time budget:** preparation 2 min; leg pattern 2 min; M1 3 min; M2/connect 4 min; comfort/reflection 2 min = **13 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Changes hands on the intended beats without inserting an extra beat or lifting the shoulders.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Do I know which hand owns each beat?” Playing—“Can the waiting hand stay prepared and easy?”
- **Practicing:** Repeat only H001 Measure 1 three times.
- **Stuck:** Alternate hands on the legs, then on C3/C4 with no second measure; use **RF-HANDS-1**.
- **Keep Playing:** Repeat H001 and say “left, right” instead of count numbers, then return to counting.

### 016 — A Two-Hand Conversation · Play

- **Primary objective:** Perform P02 as a complete written question-and-answer piece with hands alternating by measure.
- **Prerequisites:** Treble/bass C–E, quarter/half notes, and prepared hand changes.
- **Music reference:** P02 “Hello, Down There.”

**Instructional explanation**

> A musical conversation can pass an idea from one hand to the other. The top staff belongs to the right hand; the bottom staff belongs to the left. Only one hand plays in each measure here. A centered rest sign in the other staff means “this hand waits for all four beats.” Prepare both hands before the count-in.

**Demonstration:** Highlight paired measures 1/2 as question/answer. Play each hand separately, then perform all four measures. Introduce the whole-measure rest only as a full-measure waiting sign; do not compare it with a whole note or ask the learner to calculate its value.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Preview active-hand labels, first notes, and rhythm.
2. Play RH Measures 1/3 separately; play LH Measures 2/4 separately.
3. Practice the M1→M2 and M3→M4 hand changes.
4. Count in and play all P02; make one targeted repair and replay.

- **Visual/notation:** P02 paired score, active-hand highlighting on demand, measure numbers, reference audio after preparation.
- **Time budget:** preview 2 min; separate parts 3 min; transitions 3 min; two full attempts/repair 4 min; reflection 1 min = **13 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Performs the four measures with clear hand changes and an approximately steady pulse.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I follow top/right and bottom/left staves?” Playing—“Can I change hands while the count continues?”
- **Practicing:** Loop one question/answer pair, then connect the second pair.
- **Stuck:** Use **RF-HANDS-2**: play M1, count four silent beats, then M2; revisit Session 015 if switching causes an extra pause.
- **Keep Playing:** Replace Measure 4 with one written C3–E3 answer choice, then return to the score.

### 017 — A Beat of Silence · Learn

- **Primary objective:** Count and perform a quarter rest as one beat of intentional silence.
- **Prerequisites:** Quarter notes and steady four-beat pulse.
- **Music reference:** R003 A–B.

**Instructional explanation**

> A quarter rest means one beat with no note. Silence still occupies time. Keep counting through it so the next note arrives in the right place. Release the previous note before the rest; do not fill the silence with an accidental sound.

**Demonstration:** Speak “play, rest, play, play,” then count and perform R003 A. Animate the rest’s beat cell even though no key lights.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Tap four beats, leaving beat 2 silent while the voice continues.
2. Speak and tap R003 A; then play it on C4–E4.
3. Speak, tap, and play R003 B.
4. Replay A without the beat cursor.

- **Visual/notation:** Large quarter-rest symbol, four beat cells, R003 A–B, keyboard highlights only on sounding beats.
- **Time budget:** explanation/model 3 min; silent tap 2 min; two patterns 4 min; independent repeat 2 min; reflection 1 min = **12 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Leaves one full silent beat and enters the following note without losing the measure.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I explain why silence still receives a count?” Playing—“Can I release before the rest and enter afterward?”
- **Practicing:** Repeat “C4, rest, C4, rest” on four beats.
- **Stuck:** Clap only beats 1/3 while saying all four numbers; use **RF-REST-1**.
- **Keep Playing:** Insert one quarter rest into M001’s pitches, decide its exact beat, and repeat the result consistently.

### 018 — Mix the First Rhythms · Review

- **Primary objective:** Retrieve quarter notes, half notes, quarter rests, and count-ins in RH and LH contexts.
- **Prerequisites:** Sessions 005, 006, and 017; basic RH/LH reading.
- **Music reference:** R004 A–D.

**Instructional explanation**

> Pulse is the steady grid. Rhythm is the pattern of sounds and silences placed on that grid. For each card: speak the count, tap the rhythm, then play. Switching hands should not change what the rhythm means.

**Demonstration:** Compare pulse dots with R004 A, then model Speak → Tap → Play. Do not demonstrate all review cards.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Explain pulse versus rhythm in a sentence or by pointing to the correct visual.
2. Speak, tap, and play R004 A with RH and B with LH.
3. Perform C with its rest and D with its middle half note.
4. Choose one card at random for a no-hint repeat.

- **Visual/notation:** Mixed treble/bass cards, count reveal, hand labels, pulse grid.
- **Time budget:** retrieval 2 min; four cards 7 min; no-hint card 2 min; reflection 1 min = **12 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Performs at least three cards with correct total duration and explains pulse versus rhythm.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I tell the steady pulse from a changing rhythm?” Playing—“Which value or rest needs another repetition?”
- **Practicing:** Repeat only the selected value in a one-note pattern using **RF-RHY-3**.
- **Stuck:** Use four quarter-note beat boxes, then replace only beats 3–4 with one half note; revisit Session 005 or 017 as named.
- **Keep Playing:** Try R004 E or create one two-measure answer for the other hand.

### 019 — Soft and Strong, Never Forced · Learn

- **Primary objective:** Make two distinct dynamic levels while retaining physical ease.
- **Prerequisites:** Repeated quarter notes and comfortable setup.
- **Music reference:** D001.

**Instructional explanation**

> Dynamics are levels of sound. The symbol p means soft; f means strong. Strong does not mean stiff or harsh. A freely moving key can travel a little faster for a fuller sound while your shoulder, jaw, and wrist stay easy. If the keyboard is not touch-sensitive, listen to the model and practice relaxed movement instead of forcing a change it cannot make.

**Demonstration:** Required audio plus close hand-motion view for D001. Contrast useful fuller motion with an explicitly rejected tense/struck example only visually and briefly; do not encourage imitation of the unsafe version.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Listen to soft/strong samples and choose which is which.
2. On C4, play four relaxed `p` quarter notes; fully reset.
3. Play four comfortable `f` quarter notes without raising the shoulder or locking the wrist.
4. Alternate once more. If the instrument is not touch-sensitive, use a volume-neutral motion comparison.
5. Apply `p` then comfortable `f` to M001.

- **Visual/notation:** `p`/`f` symbols with words, D001, volume-consistent audio, motion close-up, portable-keyboard note.
- **Time budget:** listen/model 3 min; isolated groups 4 min; contrast repeat 2 min; melody transfer 2 min; reflection 1 min = **12 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Produces or correctly identifies two sound levels without visible strain or discomfort.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I explain p and f without calling f ‘hard’?” Playing—“Can I change the sound while my body stays easy?”
- **Practicing:** Use only one note, reset between every four-note group, and use **RF-DYN-1**.
- **Stuck:** Listen and identify only; if movement is uncomfortable, stop rather than repeat. Teacher guidance is recommended for persistent tension.
- **Keep Playing:** Choose soft or strong for M001, play it, then explain why the choice suits the melody.

### 020 — Make a Musical Sentence · Learn

- **Primary objective:** Shape a written phrase toward G4 and back to C4, then choose a constrained three-note answer.
- **Prerequisites:** M001 notes, metered rhythm, treble reading, and safe dynamic contrast.
- **Music references:** M001 metered and C001.

**Instructional explanation**

> A phrase is a short musical thought. This one climbs toward G, its destination, then settles back to C. Keep the pulse steady while letting the sound gently lead toward G and relax toward the ending. A musical answer can use different notes while keeping a clear rhythm and ending.

**Demonstration:** Play M001 flat/even once, then with a subtle destination and release. Label the contrast “same notes and rhythm, different shape.” Play C001 question plus Answer A while notation remains visible.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Read and tap M001’s two-measure rhythm.
2. Play it evenly once, then shape it toward G4 and back to C4.
3. Hear/read C001 Answer A and B; play both.
4. Choose A/B or create C using C4–E4, then play question and answer.
5. Return to M001 exactly as written to separate creativity from the model.

- **Visual/notation:** M001 treble score, destination marker over G4 without crescendo symbol, C001 choices, question/answer audio.
- **Time budget:** rhythm/read 2 min; two phrase attempts 4 min; answers 3 min; complete Q/A 2 min; reflection 1 min = **12 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Plays M001 with an audible destination/ending and performs one rhythmically valid answer ending on C4.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I identify the phrase’s destination and ending?” Playing—“Can I shape it without changing the written rhythm?”
- **Practicing:** Play only E4–G4–E4–D4–C4, first even and then gently shaped; use **RF-PHRASE-1**.
- **Stuck:** Keep all dynamics even and complete M001 accurately before adding shape; revisit Session 019 only for sound contrast.
- **Keep Playing:** Try the other answer, choose the more convincing one, and explain the choice in everyday language.

### 021 — Build the Chapter Piece · Practice

- **Primary objective:** Prepare P03 in sections and improve its Measure 6–7 hand change.
- **Prerequisites:** All pitch/rhythm/hand-change skills through Session 020.
- **Music reference:** P03 “Across the Keys.”

**Instructional explanation**

> Building a piece means solving small parts before asking for a full performance. Preview the score, tap its rhythm, prepare each hand, and practice the place where the hand changes. Repeating a whole piece is not always the quickest way to improve one difficult moment.

**Demonstration:** Show P03’s two four-measure phrases and model its preparation plan. Demonstrate Measure 6→7 slowly with the temporary cue “left, hold; left, rest; right, right.” Do not perform the whole piece before the learner previews it.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Preview hand labels, starting notes, quarter/half/rest values, and `p`/`f` plan.
2. Tap Measures 1–4, then 5–8 while counting.
3. Play RH sections, then LH sections, as listed in P03’s preparation plan.
4. Loop Measures 6–7 three calm times; stop and simplify if tension appears.
5. Connect Measures 1–4 and, separately, Measures 5–8. No full run is required.

- **Visual/notation:** Full P03 score, section cards, hand labels, measure numbers, isolated M6–7 view, rhythm/count audio.
- **Time budget:** preview 2 min; tapping 2 min; hands/sections 4 min; transition loop 3 min; two phrase attempts 2 min; reflection 1 min = **14 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Plays both four-measure sections separately and improves a specifically identified transition.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I explain why I isolate a difficult place?” Playing—“Which measure is ready, practicing, or stuck?”
- **Practicing:** Repeat only the named measure plus one neighboring measure using **RF-FINAL-1**.
- **Stuck:** Remove pitch: tap M6–7 with hand labels; then play C3 on beat 1 and C4/D4 on beats 3/4. Revisit Session 015 or 017 as appropriate.
- **Keep Playing:** Practice starting from Measure 5 and from Measure 7 so the piece does not depend on always starting at the beginning.

### 022 — Play the Complete Piece · Play

- **Primary objective:** Perform all eight measures of P03 with continuity and an intentional dynamic plan.
- **Prerequisites:** Both P03 sections and M6–7 transition prepared.
- **Music reference:** P03.

**Instructional explanation**

> A performance attempt keeps the musical thread from beginning to end. Set up silently, choose a comfortable pulse, and count in. If a small error happens, continue when you can. Afterward, name one precise repair—one measure, hand change, rhythm, or sound level—then try again.

**Demonstration:** Show a complete setup and count-in, then offer P03 reference audio. The full audio may be heard before or after the first run at learner choice, but the score remains primary.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Check bench/keyboard stability, both hand positions, first notes, and chosen tempo.
2. Count four and perform P03 from start to finish.
3. Reflect on continuity, comfort, pulse, notes/rhythm, and `p`/`f` contrast.
4. Repair exactly one place for up to two minutes.
5. Perform once more, accepting small errors while preserving the ending.

- **Visual/notation:** Responsive full score with enlarged/scroll mode, optional beat cursor, local reference recordings at practice/performance speeds.
- **Time budget:** setup/preview 2 min; first run 2 min; reflection/repair 4 min; second run 2 min; final reflection 2 min = **12 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Completes P03 with recognizable beginning/end, hand sequence, and intended soft/strong contrast.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Can I name one useful repair instead of judging the whole performance?” Playing—“Can I continue through a small error and finish comfortably?”
- **Practicing:** Work on the one selected place using **RF-FINAL-2**, then repeat only once in context.
- **Stuck:** Return to Session 021’s sections; a partial piece can remain completed-in-progress without losing history.
- **Keep Playing:** Give the piece a title of your own and perform once for a trusted listener or a private recording, only if desired.

### 023 — No-Hints Challenge · Review

- **Primary objective:** Retrieve Chapter 01 foundations before prompts appear and identify the next useful practice target.
- **Prerequisites:** Sessions 001–022 attempted.
- **Music references:** E001 D, N003 D, N005 D, R003 C, M001, and P03 excerpt.

**Instructional explanation**

> A no-hints challenge is a memory check, not an exam. Try first, then reveal the help. A missed item shows what to revisit; it does not erase completed work. Choose accuracy, pulse, and comfort over speed.

**Demonstration:** Explain the six-card format and show how Reveal records “used a hint” without marking failure. Do not demonstrate the actual answers.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Find C4 and place both five-note hand positions.
2. Identify mixed N002/N004 landmarks.
3. Speak/tap/play R003 C.
4. Sight-read N003 D and N005 D after a silent preview.
5. Hear E001 D and choose Same/Different.
6. Retrieve M001 and play P03 Measures 5–8 or the full piece if desired.

- **Visual/notation:** Shuffled task cards, independently revealable hints/answers, no timer, skill-specific review links.
- **Time budget:** setup/landmarks 2 min; rhythm 2 min; two reading lines 3 min; ear task 2 min; melody/piece excerpt 3 min; reflection 2 min = **14 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Demonstrates retained foundations and names one specific reinforcement target with evidence.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—select the least secure idea from landmarks, rhythm, reading, listening, or expression. Playing—select the least secure physical task.
- **Practicing:** Use the linked reinforcement for the selected skill, not the whole challenge again.
- **Stuck:** Reveal every answer, choose the earliest missing prerequisite in the reinforcement map, and revisit it without changing current position.
- **Keep Playing:** Shuffle the six cards and try a different order, or play P01 and P02 as a mini-review.

### 024 — Chapter 01 Milestone · Play

- **Primary objective:** Demonstrate integrated beginner musicianship and make an evidence-based next-step choice.
- **Prerequisites:** The learner chooses when to attempt; Session 023 is recommended, not a gate.
- **Music references:** K001, K002, E001, N002/N004, N003/N005, R004, and P03.

**Instructional explanation**

> This milestone asks, “What can I do now?” It is not a perfect-performance test. Use a comfortable pace and one replay where offered. You may finish the milestone, reinforce one skill, or continue and revisit later. Your completed sessions remain completed in every case.

**Demonstration:** Show the six-part order and physical setup check only. Demonstrations of individual skills are available after each first attempt, not before.

**Core physical exercise**

1. Find C4; play K001 and K002 with an approximately steady pulse.
2. Identify C4/G4 and C3/F3 in notation; read one short C–E line in each clef.
3. Tap and play one R004 rhythm containing a half note and quarter rest.
4. Hear and reproduce or identify one E001 three-note pattern.
5. Prepare and perform P03 with intended `p`/`f` contrast.
6. Complete understanding, playing, and readiness reflections for each milestone area.

- **Visual/notation:** Milestone checklist with observable behaviors, independent hints, full P03 score, audio replay; no percentage grade or locked chapter gate.
- **Time budget:** setup/patterns 2 min; landmarks/reading 3 min; rhythm 2 min; listening 2 min; P03 3 min; reflection/next step 2 min = **14 minutes**.
- **Observable outcome:** Independently finds C4; maintains pulse in a five-note pattern; reads a short line in each clef; handles quarter/half/rest rhythm; responds to a tiny heard pattern; and completes P03 with a deliberate contrast—or identifies a specific reinforcement next step for any unfinished behavior.
- **Reflection:** Understanding—“Which idea can I explain without help?” Playing—“Which musical behavior can I demonstrate consistently?” Readiness—choose Ready, Practicing, or Stuck separately from chapter completion.
- **Practicing:** Open the one named reinforcement card and schedule a later milestone revisit.
- **Stuck:** Use the reinforcement map to find the earliest prerequisite; prior completion and current position remain intact.
- **Keep Playing:** Perform P01, P02, and P03 as a three-piece mini-recital with pauses to reset.

## Coherence validation

### Skill-order audit

- Sessions 001–004 use keyboard diagrams and pulse only; staff reading begins in 007 after durations begin in 005.
- Treble reading develops from contour (007) to landmarks (008), steps (009), and a piece (010).
- LH physical setup (011) precedes LH echoes (012), bass landmarks (013), and bass reading (014).
- Hands take turns physically (015) before a two-hand score (016). No chapter task requires simultaneous notes.
- Quarter rests are formally introduced in 017, then retrieved in 018 and used in the final piece.
- Dynamics (019) and phrase direction (020) precede final-piece construction (021–022).
- Ear tasks reveal and reconnect to contour or notation; they do not replace reading.
- Every session contains a physical action at the piano or, for pulse preparation, immediately transfers tapping to the keys.

### Timing and practice audit

Every core totals 12–14 minutes, leaving small variance within the 10–15-minute target. Six Practice and three Review sessions remain substantive: they vary context, retrieve without immediate answers, diagnose a specific gap, and route to a targeted repair. Play sessions require preparation, a complete musical attempt, reflection, and one repair rather than mere repetition.

### Instrument and comfort audit

All required pitches fit C3–G4, all exercises work without pedal, and volume controls are not required. Short keyboards must contain that range. Non-touch-sensitive keyboards can complete dynamic listening and relaxed-motion work without being told to force an impossible touch response. The score/diagram presentation must work beside both an acoustic piano and a portable keyboard, with the browser acting only as a guide.

### Required expert review

A qualified piano teacher should review: hand-position and wrist language for diverse bodies and hand sizes; LH 5 on C3/RH 1 on C4 as the chapter’s default; dynamic-production guidance; every fingering and engraved score; P03’s Measure 6–7 transition; target tempos; and recorded demonstrations. An accessibility reviewer should test staff alternatives, zoom/scroll behavior, audio transcripts, touch targets, and navigation with keyboard and screen reader. These reviews are required before implementation is treated as educationally approved.

### Baseline clarification for review

The approved outline calls Session 006’s echoes “two-measure” patterns. The music specification begins with three one-measure echoes and reserves a two-measure pattern for Keep Playing, matching the core time and true-beginner load. This is a proposed interpretation, not a silent edit to the approved outline.

## Recommended first implementation slice

Implement **Session 001 (Learn, reused), Session 004 (Practice), and Session 010 (Play)** first, plus the shared repeat/revisit and Ready/Practicing/Stuck ending.

- **001** preserves the published learning experience and tests how its existing five steps are divided into essential core and Keep Playing without discarding content.
- **004** reuses its keyboard/fingering assets while testing pulse animation, an actual Practice session, a targeted repair, and the difference between completed and still-practicing.
- **010** introduces a small, complete notation-led piece and tests score readability, section preparation, optional reference audio, performance reflection, and repair without requiring left-hand or paired-staff implementation yet.
- Shared navigation must allow a learner at 010 to revisit 001 or 004 without changing the current sequential position, losing completion, or overwriting separate understanding/playing/readiness choices.
- Each state has distinct useful support: Ready offers Continue/Keep Playing; Practicing offers the named repeat; Stuck opens the simpler prerequisite and earlier-session link.

This three-session slice spans Learn → Practice → Play, keyboard diagram → pulse-guided playing → standard notation, and first exposure → retrieval → complete performance. It is large enough to test the chapter’s learning loop, local progress model, mobile use beside a physical keyboard, and non-punitive learner control. It intentionally postpones bass clef, paired staves, and final-piece coordination until these foundational interactions are validated. No new application behavior is implemented by this documentation proposal.
