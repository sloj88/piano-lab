# Chapter 01 Outline — Meet the Piano

Status: curriculum proposal, not an implementation specification
Sessions: 001–024
Chapter allocation: 24 sessions

## Chapter purpose

Chapter 01 turns the keyboard into a recognizable map, establishes healthy first movements, begins pulse and notation early, introduces the left hand without rushing coordination, and ends with a short complete two-hand piece.

The published **Your First Five Notes** lesson is Session 001 in full. Later sessions retrieve and apply its material; they do not reteach its five guided steps. If Session 001 exceeds a particular learner’s 10–15-minute capacity, its existing Reflect points are natural pause points. No new content should be hidden after such a pause.

## Entry and exit criteria

**Entry:** No musical knowledge. Learner has a physical piano or keyboard and can place the browser beside it.

**Exit milestone:** With a comfortable setup, the learner can independently find middle C; keep an approximately steady pulse while playing a five-note pattern; read a very short stepwise phrase in treble and bass clef hands separately; and perform one short two-hand piece based on alternating or shared notes with an intentional loud/soft contrast.

The milestone is diagnostic, not punitive. A learner who cannot yet demonstrate one element receives a targeted Practice session before Chapter 02 rather than being told to “try harder” or restart the chapter.

## Session-by-session sequence

| Session and title | Learning objective and type | Core physical exercise | Expected outcome | Optional **Keep Playing** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **001 — Your First Five Notes** | Navigate the keyboard, find middle C, name C–G, use RH 1–5, and play the existing melody. **Learn** | Complete the published lesson: black-key landmarks; C4–G4; RH ascending/descending; C–D–E–G–E–D–C | Finds middle C from the two-black-key landmark and plays the seven-note melody with a relaxed RH | Play the melody once softly and once at a comfortable medium sound |
| **002 — Find It Again** | Retrieve Session 001 without rereading every explanation. **Review** | Look away, then find two groups of two black keys, two Cs, middle C, and the C–G hand position; play the melody from memory, then use the hint if needed | Finds the landmarks and restarts the melody independently; identifies whether the gap is understanding or playing | Start the melody on another C and compare the higher/lower sound |
| **003 — Feel a Steady Pulse** | Distinguish an ongoing beat from a sequence of notes. **Learn** | With hands away from keys, tap an even pulse on the fallboard or leg; count 1–2–3–4; pause and restart at the same comfortable speed | Produces 16 approximately even taps while counting without rushing the last beats | Walk or sway to a favorite recording and identify its underlying pulse |
| **004 — Five Notes, One Beat Each** | Coordinate one key press with each beat. **Practice** | Place RH on C4–G4; count four preparatory beats, then play C–D–E–F–G and back, one note per beat | Plays the pattern twice without an extra pause at G or a speed-up on the descent | Repeat at a slightly slower pulse and notice whether control improves |
| **005 — One Beat or Two** | Hear and perform quarter-note and half-note durations before relying on notation. **Learn** | On middle C, alternate one-beat sounds and two-beat sounds while counting aloud; release deliberately after the correct count | Holds and releases notes for one or two beats while pulse continues | Create a four-note pattern using one- and two-beat sounds and repeat it |
| **006 — Rhythm Echoes** | Reproduce short heard/spoken patterns using one- and two-beat sounds. **Practice** | Tap, then play on C and D, several two-measure echo patterns; keep counting through held notes | Accurately echoes at least three short patterns after hearing each twice | Invent a pattern, tap it, then play it on any two C–G notes |
| **007 — The Staff Shows Shape** | Understand that the five-line staff represents relative pitch and time from left to right. **Learn** | Trace printed/on-screen contours that repeat, step up, or step down; mirror each contour on adjacent C–G keys without naming staff pitches yet | Correctly matches repeat/up/down notation to same/right/left key movement | Draw a four-note contour on paper and play a matching keyboard shape |
| **008 — Two Treble Landmarks** | Locate middle C and treble G in notation and on the keyboard. **Learn** | Find C4 from its ledger-line location and G4 on the treble-clef second line; alternate C4 and G4 with RH while naming each | Identifies both written landmarks without a mnemonic and plays the correct key | Find another C and G on the keyboard and compare octave/register |
| **009 — Read C, D, E** | Read stepwise notes outward from middle C on the treble staff. **Practice** | Preview starting note and direction; play four-note patterns using C4–D4–E4 with RH, one note per counted beat | Reads a new C/D/E stepwise pattern without letter names written over notes | Read the same pattern, then transpose its keyboard shape to F–G–A by ear/shape only |
| **010 — A Phrase from the Page** | Sustain a complete very short RH notated phrase. **Play** | Preview contour and rhythm, count in, then play an original four-measure C–E piece with quarter/half notes and a clear ending | Plays the whole phrase from notation with steady enough pulse to hear its shape | Play it again and choose a title that matches its character |
| **011 — Meet the Left Hand** | Learn LH finger numbers and a comfortable C3–G3 five-finger position. **Learn** | Find C below middle C; place LH 5–4–3–2–1 on C3–G3; play up and down slowly while checking shoulder, wrist, and fingertip comfort | Names LH fingers, finds the position, and plays the pattern without pressing or stretching unnecessarily | Play the pattern softly, listening for equal tone |
| **012 — Left-Hand Steps** | Control C3–G3 with a steady pulse. **Practice** | Count in and play LH C–D–E–F–G and back; then play short repeat/step patterns with fingers 5–1 | Plays two LH patterns at an even pulse with deliberate releases | Echo a three-note RH pattern with the equivalent LH shape one octave lower |
| **013 — Bass C and Bass F** | Recognize C3 and F3 as bass-staff landmarks in the current hand position. **Learn** | Locate written C3 (bass second space) and F3 (bass fourth line), then alternate them with LH fingers 5 and 2 after a four-count | Matches each landmark to the correct key and finger without labeling every intervening note | Find C3/F3 in two new short written patterns containing repeats |
| **014 — Read C, D, E with the Left Hand** | Read stepwise bass-clef patterns from C3. **Practice** | Preview C3, trace repeat/up/down direction, then play four-note C3–D3–E3 patterns with LH and counted quarter/half notes | Reads a new short LH pattern while keeping the hand relaxed and pulse audible | Read one pattern beginning on F3 and follow steps by interval rather than note-name recall |
| **015 — Hands Take Turns** | Keep one pulse while alternating between hands. **Learn** | Prepare LH C3 and RH C4; play LH–RH–LH–RH on four beats, then small two-note “questions” and “answers” without moving both at once | Changes hands without inserting an extra beat or lifting the shoulders | Alternate LH C–D with RH C–D for two measures |
| **016 — A Two-Hand Conversation** | Play a complete call-and-response miniature. **Play** | Learn each two-beat LH/RH phrase separately, then alternate them across four measures with a count-in | Performs the complete miniature with clear hand changes and a maintained pulse | Reverse which hand begins and listen to how the register changes the character |
| **017 — A Beat of Silence** | Treat a quarter rest as counted musical time. **Learn** | Tap four beats including a silent beat; then play C–rest–D–E while the foot or quiet count maintains pulse | Leaves the correct silence without shortening the measure or losing the next entrance | Insert one rest into a familiar C–D–E–G pattern and play it twice the same way |
| **018 — Mix the First Rhythms** | Retrieve quarter notes, half notes, quarter rests, and count-ins in varied contexts. **Review** | Speak, tap, then play mixed two-measure rhythms on one note and on C–E; alternate RH and LH examples | Performs at least three mixed rhythms and explains the difference between pulse and rhythm | Create a two-measure rhythm for the other hand to “answer” one octave away |
| **019 — Soft and Strong, Never Forced** | Produce an audible dynamic contrast through key speed without bodily tension. **Learn** | On one comfortable key, play groups of four softly and then more strongly; reset shoulders/wrist between groups; stop if discomfort appears | Makes two distinguishable dynamic levels while remaining physically comfortable | Apply soft/strong contrast to the Session 001 melody |
| **020 — Make a Musical Sentence** | Hear and shape a phrase with a destination and release. **Learn** | Play C–D–E–G–E–D–C; grow slightly toward G and relax toward the final C, breathing/releasing at the end | Produces a recognizable rise/fall shape and explains where the phrase seems to lead | Try a different shape and choose which sounds more convincing |
| **021 — Build the Chapter Piece** | Prepare the chapter piece by isolating rhythm, hands, and transitions. **Practice** | For an original 8–12-measure piece, tap the rhythm, play each hand’s part, loop the hand-change or shared-note transition, then reconnect two sections | Identifies and improves one specific problem rather than merely repeating full runs | Practice starting from the second phrase and from the final two measures |
| **022 — Play the Complete Piece** | Sustain the chapter piece with pulse and one expressive plan. **Play** | Set up silently, count in, perform the whole piece; reflect on continuity, comfort, and sound; make one targeted repair and replay | Completes the piece with recognizable beginning/end and chosen dynamic contrast, even if a small error occurs | Give the piece a title and perform it once for a trusted listener or recording |
| **023 — No-Hints Challenge** | Test retention before prompts appear. **Review** | Find middle C; identify C4/G4 and C3/F3 in notation; clap a mixed rhythm; play one RH and one LH sight-reading line; retrieve the first melody | Demonstrates retained foundations and names one specific skill needing another practice session | Mix five task cards and perform them in a new order |
| **024 — Chapter 01 Milestone** | Demonstrate integrated beginner musicianship and choose the next focus. **Play** | Complete a comfort/setup check; play RH and LH five-note patterns; read one short line in each clef; clap a rhythm; perform the chapter piece with intentional dynamics | Meets the exit milestone or receives a specific reinforcement recommendation; completes separate “understand” and “can play” reflections | Play both complete Chapter 01 pieces as a two-piece mini-recital |

## Content requirements before implementation

- A qualified piano educator must review every notated example, fingering, hand position, and the final piece.
- Rhythm examples require a defined counting system that remains consistent across later chapters.
- The original chapter piece must work on acoustic piano and common smaller keyboards without depending on sustain pedal.
- Treble and bass examples must be visually accessible and must not encode required information by color alone.
- The product must decide whether Session 001’s current length remains acceptable or whether its optional practice is visually separated from its 10–15-minute core; this outline does not modify it.
- “Approximately steady” requires a human-readable behavioral description, not microphone or MIDI scoring.
